## Part A: Factoring — The Reverse of FOIL (Warm-up)

In Worksheet 3A, you expanded $(t + 3)(t + 4)$ to get $t^2 + 7t + 12$.

Now we go backwards: given $t^2 + 7t + 12$, find the two binomials.

**The Key Insight:**

To factor $x^2 + bx + c$, find two numbers that:
- **Multiply** to give $c$ (the constant term)
- **Add** to give $b$ (the coefficient of $x$)

**Example:** Factor $x^2 + 7x + 12$

- Need two numbers that multiply to 12 and add to 7
- Factors of 12: (1,12), (2,6), (3,4)
- Which pair adds to 7? → 3 and 4
- Answer: $(x + 3)(x + 4)$

## Part E: Factoring When the Leading Coefficient ≠ 1 (12 problems)

When you have $ax^2 + bx + c$ with $a \neq 1$, factoring is trickier.

**Method: AC Method (Factor by Grouping)**

**Example:** Factor $2x^2 + 7x + 3$

1. Multiply $a \cdot c$: $2 \cdot 3 = 6$
2. Find two numbers that multiply to 6 and add to 7: → 6 and 1
3. Rewrite the middle term: $2x^2 + 6x + 1x + 3$
4. Group and factor: $2x(x + 3) + 1(x + 3)$
5. Factor out common binomial: $(2x + 1)(x + 3)$

## Part G: Solving by Factoring — Zero Product Property (12 problems)

**The Zero Product Property:**

If $A \cdot B = 0$, then $A = 0$ or $B = 0$ (or both).

This lets us solve factored equations!

**Example:** Solve $x^2 - 5x + 6 = 0$

1. Factor: $(x - 2)(x - 3) = 0$
2. Apply Zero Product Property: $x - 2 = 0$ or $x - 3 = 0$
3. Solve each: $x = 2$ or $x = 3$

## Part H: Completing the Square (10 problems)

Sometimes factoring doesn't work nicely. **Completing the square** always works.

**The Idea:** Turn $x^2 + bx$ into a perfect square by adding $\left(\frac{b}{2}\right)^2$

**Example:** Solve $x^2 + 6x = 7$

1. Take half of 6, square it: $\left(\frac{6}{2}\right)^2 = 9$
2. Add 9 to both sides: $x^2 + 6x + 9 = 7 + 9$
3. Factor left side: $(x + 3)^2 = 16$
4. Take square root: $x + 3 = \pm 4$
5. Solve: $x = -3 + 4 = 1$ or $x = -3 - 4 = -7$

## Part I: The Quadratic Formula (10 problems)

For any quadratic $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$:

$$\boxed{x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}}$$

**Example:** Solve $2x^2 + 5x - 3 = 0$

- $a = 2$, $b = 5$, $c = -3$
- $x = \frac{-5 \pm \sqrt{25 - 4(2)(-3)}}{2(2)} = \frac{-5 \pm \sqrt{25 + 24}}{4} = \frac{-5 \pm \sqrt{49}}{4} = \frac{-5 \pm 7}{4}$
- $x = \frac{-5 + 7}{4} = \frac{1}{2}$ or $x = \frac{-5 - 7}{4} = -3$

## Part J: The Discriminant — Predicting Solutions (8 problems)

The expression under the square root, $b^2 - 4ac$, is called the **discriminant**.

It tells you what kind of solutions to expect:

| Discriminant | Number of Real Solutions |
|--------------|--------------------------|
| $b^2 - 4ac > 0$ | Two different real solutions |
| $b^2 - 4ac = 0$ | One real solution (repeated) |
| $b^2 - 4ac < 0$ | No real solutions (complex numbers) |
