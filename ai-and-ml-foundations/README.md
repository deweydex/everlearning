# AI and ML Foundations

## What this course does

The mathematics of machine learning, built from nothing but Python lists and Matplotlib, followed by the machine learning itself, built from nothing but NumPy. The order of the six parts is a suggested route rather than a strict dependency: linear algebra and probability are independent of each other, calculus is short, and the two projects at the end can be taken on their own.

## What you will learn

Vectors, matrices, and matrix multiplication as composition, with a Gaussian elimination solver. Derivatives as rates of change, numerically and symbolically. Probability from sample spaces to Bayesian updating, through simulation. What a loss function is and how an algorithm walks downhill on it. What a neuron is, what a forward pass does, how backpropagation finds the gradient, and how to assemble a network that reads handwritten letters and digits. Then, in the projects, Markov chains that generate text, and hiding and finding messages in images.

## What we expect you to know already

Python through functions, lists, loops and comprehensions, and enough Matplotlib to draw a line: Tutorials 0 to 8 and 10 to 11 of Programming and Design Principles, or the equivalent. School algebra. No linear algebra, calculus, probability or machine learning.

## The parts, in the suggested order

`New_00_Your_Questions` comes before Part 0: the learner's own situations, written down before the first lesson and returned to at the end.

0. `0-Python-and-NumPy-Refresher`: NumPy, Matplotlib, and two practice notebooks on lists, copied from Programming and Design Principles for readers who have not done that course.
1. `1-Linear-Algebra`: seven notebooks; the last opens the door to Markov chains.
2. `2-Calculus`: derivatives, then integrals, then the chain, product and quotient rules, then series (building e^x, and the sigmoid, out of nothing but powers and sums).
3. `3-Probability-and-Statistics`: six notebooks and three enrichment pieces.
4. `4-Learning-a-Line`: the smallest learning algorithm, then backpropagation; a learner copy with gaps in Alternates.
5. `5-Neural-Networks`: seven notebooks from a hand-worked forward pass to the same network in scikit-learn, plus an exemplar project.
6. `6-Projects`: From 0 to Markov (ten weeks, with its course guide) and Hidden in Plain Sight (five parts, with its teacher's guide).

`Assessments` holds the integrated assessment brief, with three project options; the exemplar in Part 5 answers Option B.

Each part has a README of its own with prerequisites and the order of its notebooks. `Topic_Map.md` says which notebook teaches which idea and what it needs from earlier ones; `Glossary.md` defines the terms the course uses, in the order they first appear.

## Notes for teachers

Parts 4 and 5 are where the course pays off and are the least settled; the Alternates folders hold the versions that were passed over. The teacher's guide for Hidden in Plain Sight cites older module codes than the rest of this collection.
