## Part G: Basic "What Happens When..." (8 problems)

No calculation required — describe the behavior.

**53.** Consider $\dfrac{n}{100}$ where $n > 0$.
- (a) As $n$ gets very large?
- (b) As $n$ approaches 0?

**54.** Consider $\dfrac{100}{n}$ where $n > 0$.
- (a) As $n$ gets very large?
- (b) As $n$ approaches 0?

**55.** For $\dfrac{k + 1}{k}$ where $k > 0$:
- (a) Rewrite as a sum of two fractions.
- (b) As $k \to \infty$, what value does this approach?

**56.** For $\dfrac{t^2}{t^2 + 1}$ where $t > 0$:
- (a) Calculate when $t = 1$, $t = 10$, $t = 100$.
- (b) What value does this approach as $t \to \infty$?
- (c) Will it ever actually reach that value?

**57.** For $\dfrac{1}{1 + n}$ where $n > 0$:
- (a) As $n \to 0$?
- (b) As $n \to \infty$?

**58.** For $\dfrac{n}{n + 1}$ where $n > 0$:
- (a) As $n \to \infty$?
- (b) Is the fraction always less than 1, equal to 1, or greater than 1?

**59.** You have fraction $\dfrac{a}{b}$ where $a, b > 0$.
- (a) If you double both numerator and denominator, what happens to the value?
- (b) If you add 1 to both numerator and denominator, does the value increase, decrease, or depend on something? *(Test with $\frac{1}{4}$ and $\frac{3}{4}$)*

**60.** For $\left(1 + \dfrac{1}{n}\right)$ where $n > 0$:
- (a) Calculate when $n = 1$, $n = 10$, $n = 100$.
- (b) What value does this approach as $n \to \infty$?
- (c) Bonus: $\left(1 + \dfrac{1}{n}\right)^n$ approaches a famous number as $n \to \infty$. Any guesses?
