## Part E: Reasoning About Fraction Behavior (6 problems)

Answer each question with a brief explanation. No calculation required.

**31.** Consider the fraction $\dfrac{n}{10}$ where $n$ is a positive number.
- (a) What happens to the value of the fraction as $n$ gets larger and larger?
- (b) What value does the fraction approach as $n$ approaches 0?

**32.** Consider the fraction $\dfrac{10}{n}$ where $n$ is a positive number.
- (a) What happens to the value as $n$ gets larger and larger?
- (b) What happens to the value as $n$ gets closer and closer to 0 (but stays positive)?

**33.** For the fraction $\dfrac{x + 1}{x}$ where $x > 0$:
- (a) Rewrite this as a sum of two simpler fractions.
- (b) As $x$ gets very large (like 100, 1000, 10000...), what value does this fraction approach?
- (c) Why does this happen?

**34.** Consider $\dfrac{x^2}{x^2 + 1}$ for positive values of $x$.
- (a) Calculate the value when $x = 1$, $x = 10$, and $x = 100$.
- (b) What value does this fraction seem to approach as $x$ grows without bound?
- (c) Will the fraction ever actually reach that value? Why or why not?

**35.** You have the fraction $\dfrac{a}{b}$ where both $a$ and $b$ are positive.
- (a) If you double both the numerator and denominator, what happens to the value?
- (b) If you add 1 to both the numerator and denominator, does the value increase, decrease, or stay the same? (Hint: test with $\frac{1}{2}$ and $\frac{3}{4}$)
- (c) Can you explain the pattern from part (b)?

**36.** The expression $\dfrac{1}{1 + \frac{1}{x}}$ can be simplified.
- (a) Simplify this expression to a single fraction.
- (b) As $x$ approaches infinity, what does the simplified expression approach?
- (c) As $x$ approaches 0 (from the positive side), what happens to the value?
